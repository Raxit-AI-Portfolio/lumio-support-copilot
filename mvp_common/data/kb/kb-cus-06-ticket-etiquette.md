Good tickets get faster answers. This article explains how to write one and what the priorities mean.

## Writing a good ticket

Give one problem per ticket. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened. Add the workspace name and, if you can, a screenshot. Do not include passwords or secret key values in a ticket.

## Priorities

### Low
Questions and small requests that do not block work.

### Normal
Something is not working as described but there is a workaround.

### High
An important feature is broken for many people and there is no workaround.

### Urgent
The whole workspace is down or data may be lost. Urgent is available on the Business tier only.

## What happens next

You get an automatic confirmation with a ticket number. A support agent replies within the target of your plan. You can add comments to the ticket by replying to the email. The ticket closes after you confirm that the problem is solved or after seven days without a reply from you.

## Reopening

A closed ticket can be reopened within 14 days by replying to it.
